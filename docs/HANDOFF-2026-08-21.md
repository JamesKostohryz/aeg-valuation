## WHAT IS OPEN, IN THE ORDER I WOULD DO IT

### 1. The company leg of the historical reconstruction — and James's scoping question

He asked, and it is the right question: *"We do have this historical data going back to 1960 and
even further back, but I wonder if all of that is necessary."*

**Probably not, and there is a cheap test that settles it.** The company premium needs two things
at each historical date:

- **the numerator** — that company's own `semidev_i`. Needs only THAT company's price history,
  which you have for any company you are valuing. Not a problem.
- **the denominator** — `capw_avg_semidev`, the cap-weighted average across the universe. This is
  the piece that appears to need the whole index's history and historical membership.

**The denominator is a market-level aggregate, not a per-company one**, and there are three tiers:

| tier | what it needs | reach |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | nothing historical at all — estimate `capw_avg_semidev` from the MARKET semi-deviation already reconstructed to 1929, via a ratio calibrated on the modern overlap | 1929 |
| **B** | a fixed panel of 50–100 long-lived large caps | ~1960 |
| **C** | full universe + historical index membership | ~1960, most work |

**Do tier A's test first, and it is one afternoon.** `capw_avg_semidev` is the average of
*residual* semi-deviations; the market series is the *index* semi-deviation. Their ratio is a
dispersion/average-correlation statistic. Compute both monthly over 2000–2026 from data you
already have and ask whether the ratio is stable. **If it is, the denominator comes for free back
to 1929 and no universe history is needed at all.** If it drifts, you know by how much and
whether tier B closes it.

**Pre-register before measuring.** State in advance what "stable enough" means — I would propose
that the ratio's standard deviation must be under 15% of its mean, and that using a fixed ratio
must not move a reconstructed company premium by more than 25bp at any date in the overlap.

## THE FIVE LEGS — coverage measured 2026-08-21, and what the prior evidence says

| leg | source | coverage | prior evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Semi-deviation, 1y/2y blend | `idio/semidev.py`, production | **99%** (499/503) | The incumbent. Beaten head-to-head by leg 2 within sector. |
| 2. Corporate bond spread | `outputs/issuer_widen_latest.csv`, `s1_pp` | **74%** (372/503) | **The best-evidenced result in the whole workstream.** t = −7.28, replicated on a doubled universe, 0 of 500 permutation draws matched it. |
| 3. Put-option implied vol | `outputs/.svix_cache`, 3,350 chains | **53%** — ~47% at 365d, ~80% at 180d | **Failed out of sample**: 35 of 90 vs a flat ERP, and a random reshuffle scored *better* (p = 1.000). Rank correlation with leg 1: **+0.778**. |
| 4. Industry risk rank | being built — see below | 100% once groups exist | New. |
| 5. Within-industry rank | needs the groups | 100% once groups exist | New. |

**Only 221 of 503 names carry legs 1–3 together.** The missing-data rule is therefore the most
consequential design choice in the build, not an afterthought: score on available legs, publish
`n_legs` on every row, refuse below two, never impute one leg from another, and **measure the
coverage bias on the 221 four-leg names** by re-scoring them on the two legs a small company
would have.

**Two things to keep saying out loud:** put-option IV failed its own out-of-sample test in this
project, and it correlates 0.778 with semi-deviation — so equal weighting does not give five
independent votes, it gives roughly three and a half, tilted toward volatility. Build it as
specified; report its marginal contribution.

## WHERE THE WORK STOPPED — industry groupings, two-stage

**The finding that shaped the design:** EODHD carries **134 industries across ~800 names, a median
of four names each**. Only **31 of 134** could field six names in sixty months, so 103 fall into
catch-all buckets. **Merging on measured risk distance cannot be step one, because the measurement
requires the merge to have already happened.**

**Stage 1 — structural consolidation, WITHIN sector, on business similarity.** The vendor's labels
are already hierarchical (`Oil & Gas E&P` / `Drilling` / `Midstream`; `Banks - Regional` /
`Diversified`; `REIT - Office` / `Retail`; `Software - Application` / `Infrastructure`).
**A 43-group table is proposed in `RESULTS-Industry-Groupings-Stage1-2026-08-21.md`, smallest
n = 6, median n ≈ 14. IT AWAITS JAMES'S SIGN-OFF AND THAT IS THE NEXT ACTION.**

**Stage 2 — risk-distance clustering, CROSS-SECTOR ALLOWED.** Built, committed and demonstrated.
The criterion was fixed in code before it was run: RMS difference between two groups' monthly risk
ratios, average-linkage agglomerative, threshold expressed in **basis points of cost of equity**
rather than as a correlation cutoff.

James's instruction of 2026-08-21 removed the within-sector constraint — *"individual stock and/or
industries can be taken out of their sectors… some utilities and industrials could be merged"* —
and it changes the answer at once. At 50bp on the 31 measurable industries, three groups span
sectors, and the first is his own example arriving unprompted:

## STILL OPEN, AND ONE OF THEM IS BIGGER THAN IT LOOKS

**The market ERP is not reconciled.** Three published numbers for the same object:
`market_coe_history.csv` says the 1-year market ERP is **5.304%**; the Option B curve
(`real-yields/history/TODAY_forward_curve_latest.csv`, the basis James confirmed live valuations
use) says **3.948%**. **1.36pp apart, today.** Every score-based ERP is a multiple of one of them.
The fix is the one the bridge was designed for and says so in its own header: feed the
reconstructed VIX-equivalent through the Option B construction instead of the Martin bound.

Also open: the refusal message names the scenario but never the ticker; size-weighting the issuer
curve fit (GATED); the financials restatement double-subtracts a bank's interest expense
(JPMorgan's economic net income comes out −$14.5bn against a reported +$55.7bn); Objective 4,
behind its pre-registration — do not build it.
